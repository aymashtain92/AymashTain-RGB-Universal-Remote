Those MR Star controllers are the problem - they are all generic Bluetooth boxes with a tiny mic on them.

They don't listen to the actual music from your phone/PC, they listen to room noise with that mic, then they have to send the color change over Bluetooth which only does like 10 commands per second. That's why it feels 0.3-0.5s late and looks bad, especially with 3 separate strips. All those generic Bluetooth music strips work the same way - built-in high-sensitivity microphone that changes color with the rhythm. 

Yes, you can control all 3 from your PC together with ZERO mic lag. The trick is to stop using the mic and use desktop audio directly.

You have 2 levels of fix:

1. Quick fix - no new hardware
Inside MR Star app:

Switch mic source from Device Mic to Phone Mic if you see it, and put your phone close to the speaker
Turn Sensitivity up to 80-100%
Turn off power saving / Bluetooth battery optimization for the app
Keep the 3 controllers close to each other
It will still lag, but less.

2. The real fix - PC audio capture with WLED + LedFx[recommended]
This is what everyone uses for music sync. LedFx is a network based LED effect engine designed to deliver advanced real-time audio effects. It captures what your PC is actually playing, not the room, and sends it over WiFi to the strips. All 3 strips become perfectly in sync. 

What you need to check first:
Look at your strip connector.

4 pins (R G B + 12V): Analog 5050 RGB - you need an analog WLED controller
3 pins (5V Data GND): Digital WS2812B / WS2811 - you need a digital WLED controller
90% of the cheap MR Star strips are 4-pin RGB.

Shopping list (cheap in Egypt):

1x ESP32 board OR better, a ready controller: Athom LS4P RGB or QuinLED-Analog - about 400-700 EGP on Noon Egypt / Amazon Egypt. Buy one that can handle 3 strips or buy 3 cheap ESP32s, they will sync anyway.
Or if you want zero soldering: GLEDOPTO WiFi RGB Controller that works with Tuya / Smart Life - it supports WiFi and is much faster than Bluetooth.
Keep your existing 12V power supply, don't power 3x 5m strips from PC USB.
Setup:

Flash WLED: Go to install.wled.me on Chrome, plug ESP32 via USB, click Install. Takes 1 minute.
Wire it: Power supply -> controller -> strips. If you have 3 separate strips, you can wire all 3 R to R, G to G, B to B on one big controller if your power supply is strong enough.
Connect to WiFi: WLED will create a WiFi network, you join it and set your home WiFi. It will give you an IP like 192.168.1.45
On PC, install LedFx: Download from ledfx.app
In LedFx -> Add Device -> WLED, put the IP. Add all 3 if you have 3 controllers.
In LedFx Audio settings -> Audio Input -> set to Loopback / What U Hear / Stereo Mix - this is the key. Now it's listening to Spotify/YouTube directly, not a mic.
Choose effect Energy or Spectrum and hit play.
Result: ∼30-50ms latency instead of 300ms+, and all 3 strips act as one big visualizer.

Other PC software options:

SignalRGB / OpenRGB: Great if you have gaming RGB too. They can control all your RGB devices from one app, but they don't natively support these generic MR Star Bluetooth controllers. You'd need a bridge like RGB-PC python script for Bluetooth, it's janky. WLED + LedFx is 10x more reliable. 
If you stay with Bluetooth, you can control it from PC with a Python script that scans Bluetooth and syncs to screen/music, but it's still limited by Bluetooth speed.

Got it - thanks for correcting me. You're using the controller's own mic / DEVICE MIC mode for that dance pattern you like, not the phone mic.

And you're right, that's an important difference:

Device mic mode = has all the cool patterns, but the little chip on the controller has to listen, process, then change the LEDs. That's where the delay comes from.
Phone mic mode = phone does the processing so it's almost instant, but yeah, they only give you like 2-3 basic patterns.
So if you don't want to buy anything new, clustering all 3 controllers together is actually the smartest free fix. If they're in 3 different corners of the room, they hear the music at different times and volumes, so they will never look in sync.

Your plan to cut and extend the wire between the USB plug and the controller will work - that's low voltage 5V, so it's safe to do. Just do it right so you don't get dim colors or restarts:

How to extend it properly
You have: USB plug -> 2 wires -> little controller box -> 4 wires -> LED strip

You're extending the 2-wire side. That's the easiest side.

Unplug everything. Don't cut while powered.
Cut in the middle. Inside you'll see red = 5V and black/white = GND.
Use thicker wire than what they gave you. The thin stock wire drops voltage fast. If you go longer than 1.5-2m, use at least 20-22 AWG (like from an old USB cable or speaker wire). Thin earphone wire will make them dim / reboot.
Solder + heat shrink, don't just twist and tape. If you twist, the 3 strips will flicker when you move the wire.
Keep all 3 extensions roughly the same length. If one is 50cm and one is 3m, that long one will be dimmer.
Don't power all 3 strips from one laptop USB port. One USB port = 0.5A, one 5m strip can pull 1-2A. Use a powered USB hub or 3 separate 5V 2A phone chargers. Otherwise when the bass hits they all try to go white and the voltage crashes, which looks like lag.
Better if you have them lying around: Don't cut at all, just use a regular USB extension cable. Same result, no soldering, and no warranty loss.

How to get them as tight as possible once they're together
Put the 3 little boxes right next to each other, mic holes facing the speaker, not behind the TV or under fabric. That mic hole is tiny - if it's covered it adds delay.
In the MR Star app, group them. Usually you can long-press > Create Group > select all 3. Then set the sensitivity and pattern once for the group so they don't fight each other.
Set the same sensitivity for all 3. If one is on 50% and one on 90%, they'll react differently.
Put the cluster near your speaker/subwoofer, not across the room. Bass is what triggers them best.
They will still not be 100% frame-perfect because each box processes on its own, but clustered like this they will be much closer than before.

Honest take: if that dance pattern you love is only in Device Mic mode, you will always have a little delay - that's just how those generic boxes work. If you ever want zero delay for that same pattern, the only free way is to control the boxes directly from your PC's audio with Bluetooth, instead of using their mic. Your controllers are GATT-DEMO devices. That's the real name they broadcast over Bluetooth. You can control them directly from your PC over Bluetooth without the phone app at all:

gatttool -b FF:23:12:22:XX:XX --char-write-req -a 0x0003 -n bc01010055 - off
gatttool -b FF:23:12:22:49:70 --char-write-req -a 0x0003 -n bc0406000003e8000055 - red 

That bc...55 protocol is exactly what the MR Star app uses. So you don't need new hardware.

You have 3 options on PC, from easiest to best for music:

1. LumaBLE - try this first [Windows GUI, no coding]
It's a desktop app that discovers compatible BLE LED controllers nearby and gives them a proper desktop interface: colour, brightness, power, effects, scenes, screen and audio sync 

And it has Screen and audio sync — make the strip follow desktop content or music in real time 

Close MR Star app on your phone completely - BLE strips only allow 1 connection at a time

Then this controls all 3 at once from your PC - no mic lag because PC sends the color directly:

```python
import asyncio
from mr_star_ble import MrStarLight

async def main():
    # it will scan for all GATT-DEMO devices
    print("Scanning for your 3 strips...")
    devices = await MrStarLight.discover_all(timeout=10)

    for d in devices:
        await d.connect()
        print(f"Connected to {d.address}")

    # Example - set all 3 to red, synced
    for d in devices:
        await d.set_rgb_color((255, 0, 0))
        await d.set_brightness(1.0)
        await d.set_power(True)

    # keep them on
    await asyncio.sleep(9999)

asyncio.run(main())
